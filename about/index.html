Why I built Noodle

Hi, I'm Jessica.
I've always been curious about people.

Where it started

When I was little, I mowed lawns for the seniors in town. While I did enjoy the mowing part, it was when I finished and got to sit with the person over a glass of water (and oftentimes a cookie) that had the biggest impact.

The stories about their life, their interests, their children. It meant something to me, and even more to them. Here's this little girl who's taking the time to listen, and curious enough to ask questions.

It seems as technology has allowed for more "connections," this human-to-human part has gotten a bit lost.

The hello game

My husband and I have a running game when we walk down the street: how many people can we get to say hello? Where we live, it's less than a 50% success rate. It sounds small, but I think about it a lot.

We're all here, moving through the same world, carrying our own stories, experiences, ideas and things we could teach each other. Yet it's easier to stay heads down and move right past one another.

Connection takes intention

I've seen how much the people around us can shape our lives. They open doors. They show us a way in. They teach us something we didn't know. They help us feel like we belong. Sometimes they simply remind us that we're not doing this alone.

But connection takes intention. We're busy. We're heads down. Networking can feel awkward and transactional. And most of us aren't great at remembering all the little things someone tells us, even when those details are exactly what helps a relationship deepen.

Noodle is my attempt to make that easier. To help us learn from each other, show up for each other, and keep the connections that matter alive.

Remembering someone is a way of saying, I see you. And both sides benefit.

When we look a little closer, we find more to learn. More ways to help. More reasons to reach out. And maybe a few more people who change our lives.

That's what I want Noodle to help create.
