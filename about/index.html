Why I built Noodle

Hi, I'm Jessica.
I've always been curious about people.

When I was little, I mowed lawns for the seniors in town. I liked the mowing fine, but the best part came after, sitting with them over a glass of water (and often a cookie). They'd tell me about their lives, their interests, their kids. Those conversations meant something to both of us.

I still think about that. My husband and I have a running game when we walk down the street: how many people can we get to say hello? Where we live, it's less than half. It sounds small, but it stays with me. We're all moving through the same world, carrying stories and ideas we could share, and it's easier to keep our heads down and walk right past each other.

The people around us shape our lives. They open doors, teach us things we didn't know, and remind us we're not doing this alone. But connection takes intention. We're busy. Networking can feel awkward. And most of us aren't great at remembering the little things someone tells us, even though those details are often what help a relationship grow.

Noodle is my attempt to make that easier. To help us show up for each other, learn from each other, and keep the connections that matter alive. And maybe find a few more people who change our lives.

P.S. A poem that inspired me: "Please Use AI" by Shawn Smucker. And if Noodle helps you reconnect with someone, I'd love to hear about it: support@noodleit.me
